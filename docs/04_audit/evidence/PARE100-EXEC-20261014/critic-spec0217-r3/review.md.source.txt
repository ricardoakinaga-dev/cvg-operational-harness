## Shared lifecycle is connected and implementable

SPEC123–137 specifies one registry per process and one slot reserved before awaiting claim. This closes the claim-in-acquisition shutdown interval. It names the actual future binding: new claimExecutions options in both workers and job inputs, root construction in openPostgresControlledConnection, trusted buildEffect(claimContext), real createPostgresControlledHandlers and real createControlledDispatch. The event remains OutboxEffect(event) inside the real ACK boundary; the registry does not introduce another runtime executor.

That wiring matches the supplied current sites:

- postgres-controlled.ts97–162 creates the shared pool/repositories and controlled worker;171–215 constructs the continuous worker from the same connection root. These are the concrete places to build/pass the new registry and expose it to main.
- controlled-worker.ts50–84 and continuous-worker.ts569–575 currently acquire claims differently. The new shared claimNextForExecution is specified for both, returning the corresponding slot; completeClaimedOutboxEvent rejects absent/foreign slots in the opt-in branch. It does not claim a second time.
- jobs/process-outbox-event.ts133–169 currently owns ACK and catch/fail. R3 keeps job ownership of those decisions, uses ackClaim/failClaim, and places beforeFinalAck outside DB locks. Calling handle.stopAndJoin there waits only its renewal, avoiding registry.stopAndDrain self-wait.
- continuous-worker.ts323–383 currently has setInterval and a synchronous timer-only stop;430–460 installs it per job. SPEC137 explicitly bypasses that path in the new branch. The compositor does not add another inner timer.

The handle transitions through STARTING/OUTER_ACTIVE/PAIRED_ACTIVE/STOPPING/STOPPED, cannot restart, and serializes attachInner with any outer-only tick. First paired renewal is required before factory/dispatch. S3/already-completed/marker replay uses only transport and never attaches/renews a closed inner execution. At most one real inFlight renewal promise belongs to a handle, through checkout, statement, commit/rollback and cleanup.

## Stop/join, clocks and pool

The proposed runner is a concrete required refactor, not claimed current functionality. `tenant-scoped-postgres.ts:157–215` currently performs operation→tenant cleanup→COMMIT; it has no stop guard there. SPEC142–144 identifies that exact gap and proposes withTenantTransactionInternal/runFencedRenewalTransaction plus queryRenewal checks after awaits and immediately before COMMIT, including after tenant cleanup. Existing public withTenantTransaction stays unbound-compatible. It does not invent AbortSignal support in the current query signature.

The timeout contract separates immediate local admission abort from actual SQL completion: lock500ms, statement1000ms, connection1000ms and local1500ms watchdog do not authorize detaching inFlight work. stopAndJoin tracks the real promise through rollback/cleanup/release; a late checkout must release/destroy without starting renewal SQL. Cleanup failure destroys the client. Ambiguous commit/real SQL or ownership failure remains a veto even when healthy shutdown races it. A timeout/unknown join cannot be reported as a healthy stop or ACK.

SPEC145–148 makes the first terminal wrapper close local entry and requestStop synchronously, then join before terminal IO/close PG. It retains immutable settlement/ACK refs but permanently detaches both renewal targets; there is no outer renewal loop after close. Remaining lease is checked at close/completion/ACK; insufficient time yields safe failure/redelivery instead of post-close renewal. This preserves S3 replay without claiming all finalization delays fit10s.

Current main.ts360–365 only waits controlled drain before pool.end; current continuous-worker.ts733–741 may fail active handlers in its shutdown release loop. SPEC147/HOW413,421 explicitly replace these behaviors only in the opt-in route: close slots, abort handles, join renewals and drain the unique jobs before pool.end, with no parallel legacy fail. Unconfirmed drain is an error, not STOPPED success. Pool4/concurrency1 accommodates the retained outer ACK client, foreground inner transaction, single renewal transaction and one operation/cleanup client; no inner transaction may checkout through another pool-backed wrapper while holding those locks.

Acceptance125–130 names the corresponding counterexamples: missing/dual timer, claim during shutdown, blocked checkout/query/commit, late ACK, failure-versus-expected-cancel race, dirty client, self-wait and legacy parallel fail. It requires real inFlight join rather than a Promise.race that only stops waiting. These are material BUILD checks already included in the candidate, not additional design gaps or completed verification.
